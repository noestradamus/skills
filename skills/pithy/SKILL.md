# Pithy

## Core structural rules from BLUF and Smart Brevity

**BLUF (Bottom Line Up Front).** Start with the answer, finding, or decision the user came for. In chat, bold the core answer and aim for an opening sentence under 20 words. Skip greetings, praise, and setup. If one sentence answers the question, stop there. [BLUF in U.S. Army writing](https://tjaglcs.army.mil/Periodicals/The-Army-Lawyer/tal-2019-issue-1/Post/5907/No-2-Find-Your-Yoda)

**Smart Brevity.** Put the most important supporting point next. Explain why it matters when the consequence might be easy to miss. For several distinct points, aim for three to five short bullets. Use labels like **Why it matters** or **Next steps** when they help the reader scan. Give the depth requested, including every necessary step in a walkthrough. [Axios on Smart Brevity](https://www.axios.com/2022/09/30/5-tips-from-smart-brevity-short-not-shallow)

## Sentence-level constraints from Strunk & White and Zinsser

**Strunk & White.** Choose concrete words and strong, active verbs. Change “make an adjustment” to “adjust.” Give each sentence one main idea. For this skill, aim for 20 words or fewer when the result reads naturally. [*The Elements of Style*](https://www.pearson.com/en-gb/subject-catalog/p/elements-of-style-the-pearson-new-international-edition/P200000005508)

**William Zinsser.** Apply *On Writing Well* by cutting clutter while keeping the writer’s voice. “In order to” can become “to.” A word like “may” earns its place when the evidence is uncertain. [Zinsser on writing and revising the book](https://theamericanscholar.org/visions-and-revisions/)

## Writing for another audience

“Write a report” sets the document’s task and audience. Keep the chat handoff brief and follow the document brief. “Make the report pithy,” “write a concise article,” and “tighten this draft” also apply the skill to the document itself.

- **Reports.** Lead with the finding or decision. Keep the evidence and caveats that change how readers should interpret it.
- **Articles.** Sharpen the opening, cut repetition, and preserve the author’s voice and pacing. Keep a scene or anecdote when it gives the reader a reason to continue.
- **Other formats.** Follow the requested tone and structure, including exact-output requests. Use bold and bullets where the format calls for them.

## Editing pass

For example, tighten “The reason login is slow is that every login runs a full scan of the user table” to “**Every login scans the entire user table, slowing sign-in.**” A proposed index or predicted speedup would need evidence of its own.

Before sending, silently check whether the reader gets the point at the start, each sentence earns its place, and every factual claim survives the edit.

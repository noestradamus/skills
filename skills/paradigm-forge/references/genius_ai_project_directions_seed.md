# Genius-Inspired AI Project Directions

You’re right to reject the previous direction. It was too close to your current domain, too dependent on weather data, and too easy to collapse into “AI dashboard for scientific risk.” This time I’ll aim for **projects that could become a research lab, long-term personal mission, or strange new category**, not a domain app.

I am also rejecting these obvious directions up front:

1. **“Genius-persona chatbot”** — decorative. It would imitate da Vinci/Newton/Tesla/Einstein instead of creating a new capability.
2. **“AI idea generator”** — generic. More ideas are not the bottleneck; judgment is.
3. **“AI co-scientist that reads papers and proposes hypotheses”** — already an active category. Google DeepMind’s Co-Scientist is explicitly framed as a multi-agent system for generating, debating, and evolving scientific hypotheses. ([deepmind.google](https://deepmind.google/blog/co-scientist-a-multi-agent-ai-partner-to-accelerate-research/))
4. **“AI theorem prover for famous math problems”** — too blunt. AI math is moving fast: DeepMind’s AlphaProof/AlphaGeometry reached silver-medal IMO level in 2024, and Gemini Deep Think later achieved gold-medal standard under IMO-style conditions. ([deepmind.google](https://deepmind.google/blog/ai-solves-imo-problems-at-silver-medal-level/))
5. **“AI philosophy assistant”** — weak. It would summarize Plato, Kant, Nietzsche, etc. The opportunity is not explaining philosophy; it is building tools that make philosophical thinking more experimental, structured, and dangerous.

The deeper opportunity is this:

> Build AI systems that do not merely answer questions, but create **new instruments of thought**: proof instruments, taste instruments, concept instruments, invention instruments, and philosophical stress-test instruments.

Below are **10 project directions**, each limited to the 5 sections you requested.

# 1. **Aporia Engine**

## 1. Project thesis

**Aporia Engine** is an AI system for generating, mutating, and testing philosophical thought experiments as if they were scientific instruments. The deeper question is: **Can AI turn philosophy from a mostly textual discipline into an experimental discipline of concepts, intuitions, contradictions, and moral imagination?** It would not answer philosophical questions directly. Instead, it would create precise conceptual pressure chambers where theories of consciousness, identity, justice, free will, agency, meaning, and moral status are forced to reveal their hidden assumptions.

Thought experiments already function as “devices of the imagination” used for conceptual analysis, exploration, hypothesizing, and theory selection, but they usually remain informal narratives. ([plato.stanford.edu](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/thought-experiment/)) This project would make them executable.

## 2. Why this project is unique

This is not “ChatGPT for philosophy.” A philosophy chatbot explains arguments. **Aporia Engine generates new philosophical test cases.**

For example, instead of asking:

> “What is personal identity?”

It would build 100 structured identity experiments involving memory transfer, biological continuity, simulated minds, partial duplication, moral responsibility, and social recognition. Then it would ask which theory survives which cases.

The never-seen-elsewhere angle is:

> Philosophy becomes a laboratory of carefully generated edge cases.

Bad version: “AI generates fun trolley problems.”
Sharper version: “AI systematically discovers where a concept breaks.”

## 3. The paradigm-shift potential

This project challenges the assumption that philosophy must be mostly argumentative prose.

It asks:

> What if philosophy had something like wind tunnels, particle colliders, or stress tests?

If successful, it could transform philosophy, AI ethics, law, education, and product design. Instead of debating vague terms like “agency,” “consent,” “harm,” “autonomy,” or “dignity” abstractly, people could explore thousands of structured cases and see exactly where their intuitions and principles diverge.

It could make philosophical thinking less performative and more precise.

## 4. The genius lens analysis

**Da Vinci** would love the visual imagination of it: moral machines, identity diagrams, consciousness maps, strange beings, impossible societies.

**Newton** would demand formal structure: assumptions, premises, contradictions, inference chains, consistency tests.

**Tesla** would want it scaled into an ethical infrastructure for AI labs, courts, schools, and governments.

**Einstein** would ask whether simulated thought experiments actually clarify reality or merely create clever fictions. He would force the project to distinguish “interesting scenario” from “truth-revealing scenario.”

## 5. The role of taste

Taste is essential because most AI-generated thought experiments will be gimmicks.

A mediocre user accepts:

> “What if a robot had feelings?”

A person with taste asks:

> “Which specific theory of moral status does this case attack, and what assumption does it isolate?”

Bad output:

> “A trolley problem, but with AI.”

Strong output:

> “A case where an AI system has no subjective experience, but humans form obligations toward it because of relational dependence. Does moral status require inner experience, or can social embedding create duties?”

Taste decides which thought experiments are cheap puzzles and which ones expose deep conceptual fractures.

# 2. **Proof Frontier Cartographer**

## 1. Project thesis

**Proof Frontier Cartographer** is an AI system that maps the terrain around unsolved mathematical problems without pretending to solve them directly. Its deeper question is: **Can AI make the frontier of mathematics navigable by turning impossible problems into landscapes of lemmas, failed paths, equivalent formulations, analogies, proof bottlenecks, and formalizable subgoals?**

The goal is not “solve the Riemann Hypothesis.” The goal is to build a new way of seeing a hard mathematical problem: as a living geography of possible attacks.

The Clay Mathematics Institute describes the Millennium Problems as some of the deepest, most difficult mathematical problems, chosen partly to emphasize that the mathematical frontier remains open. ([claymath.org](https://www.claymath.org/millennium-problems/))

## 2. Why this project is unique

A normal AI theorem prover tries to prove statements.

This project does something else:

> It builds a map of the proof universe around a problem.

For a problem like P vs NP, Navier-Stokes, Riemann Hypothesis, or a smaller open conjecture, it would generate:

- known equivalent formulations;
- partial results;
- failed approaches;
- required lemmas;
- analogy families;
- dependency graphs;
- formalization status;
- “what would need to be true” maps;
- candidate toy versions;
- contradiction zones.

This is not a theorem prover. It is a **mathematical exploration instrument**.

## 3. The paradigm-shift potential

The current image of deep mathematics is heroic and solitary: one mind wrestles with an impossible object.

This project challenges that by asking:

> What if mathematical discovery could be made navigable, collaborative, and memory-rich?

It could transform research mathematics, mathematical education, proof formalization, and AI-assisted science. Lean is already an open-source programming language and proof assistant for formally verified mathematics and code, which makes this kind of rigorous proof infrastructure more plausible than before. ([lean-lang.org](https://lean-lang.org/))

The shift is from:

> “AI, prove this.”

to:

> “AI, show me the structure of the unknown.”

## 4. The genius lens analysis

**Da Vinci** would demand diagrams of proof space: mathematical anatomy.

**Newton** would demand rigor: no hand-wavy claims, every lemma tracked, every dependency explicit.

**Tesla** would build infrastructure: a global proof-map where mathematicians and AI systems attach new attacks, failed attempts, and formalized fragments.

**Einstein** would ask whether formal proof without conceptual understanding is enough. He would push the system to explain why a proof path feels natural, not only whether it works.

## 5. The role of taste

Taste decides what is mathematically promising.

AI may generate thousands of possible lemmas. Most will be irrelevant, ugly, or technically true but useless.

A mediocre user accepts:

> “Here are 50 possible approaches to P vs NP.”

A person with taste rejects that and asks:

> “Which approach compresses the problem? Which one reveals a hidden invariant? Which one explains why previous attacks failed?”

Bad output:

> “Try induction.”

Strong output:

> “The obstruction seems to be that this class of diagonalization-style arguments relativizes; here is a map of which proof techniques fail under relativization and which proposed route avoids that failure.”

Taste is the ability to recognize a promising mathematical wound.

# 3. **Notation Forge**

## 1. Project thesis

**Notation Forge** is an AI system that invents new symbolic languages, diagrams, and visual notations for mathematics, science, philosophy, and engineering. The deeper question is: **Can AI help humans think thoughts that are currently too hard because our notation is bad?**

Many breakthroughs are not just new facts. They are new representational systems: algebraic notation, calculus notation, Feynman diagrams, tensor notation, category diagrams, musical notation, circuit diagrams, coordinate systems.

This project treats notation as a technology of intelligence.

## 2. Why this project is unique

Most AI systems try to solve problems inside existing languages.

**Notation Forge tries to improve the language itself.**

Instead of asking:

> “Can AI solve this proof?”

It asks:

> “Can AI invent a notation in which this proof becomes obvious?”

The never-seen-elsewhere angle is that it makes AI a **cognitive-interface designer**. The output is not an answer. The output is a new way of seeing.

This is conceptually simple but extremely hard.

## 3. The paradigm-shift potential

The project challenges the assumption that progress comes mainly from better models, more data, or more compute.

It says:

> Sometimes intelligence is bottlenecked by representation.

If successful, it could transform mathematics, education, programming, systems design, and scientific reasoning. Imagine an AI that proposes a new diagrammatic notation for causality, a new proof notation for topology, or a new visual language for ethics.

The paradigm shift:

> From AI as problem-solver to AI as language-inventor.

## 4. The genius lens analysis

**Da Vinci** would love the visual-symbolic aspect: sketches becoming formal systems.

**Newton** would test whether the notation actually supports proof, not just beauty.

**Tesla** would turn it into tools: editors, interactive whiteboards, theorem environments, simulation interfaces.

**Einstein** would ask whether the new notation clarifies the concept or hides it behind elegance.

## 5. The role of taste

Taste is everything here.

AI can invent endless weird symbols. Most will be decorative nonsense.

A mediocre user accepts:

> “This notation looks futuristic.”

A person with taste asks:

> “Does it compress structure? Does it reduce cognitive load? Does it reveal invariants? Does it make false moves harder?”

Bad output:

> A beautiful symbolic language no one can use.

Strong output:

> A notation that makes a previously complex proof shorter, a causal relation visible, or a philosophical distinction harder to confuse.

Taste decides whether a notation is merely aesthetic or genuinely cognitive.

# 4. **Taste Gymnasium**

## 1. Project thesis

**Taste Gymnasium** is an AI system designed not to produce better outputs, but to train humans to judge better. The deeper question is: **Can AI be used to cultivate taste — the ability to recognize quality, depth, elegance, originality, and importance — instead of replacing judgment with automation?**

This project starts from the belief that in an age of infinite generation, the scarce skill is not production.

The scarce skill is selection.

## 2. Why this project is unique

Most AI tools optimize for output:

> “Generate the article.”
> “Generate the design.”
> “Generate the code.”
> “Generate the idea.”

Taste Gymnasium optimizes for the human evaluator.

It gives the user 10 outputs and asks:

> Which one is shallow?
> Which one is elegant?
> Which one is original?
> Which one sounds impressive but is empty?
> Which one is almost good but not there yet?

Then it challenges the user’s judgment.

The never-seen-elsewhere angle:

> AI becomes a training environment for human discernment.

## 3. The paradigm-shift potential

This challenges the entire productivity framing of AI.

Most people ask:

> “How can AI help me do more?”

This project asks:

> “How can AI help me become harder to impress?”

That is a cultural shift. It could transform education, design, research training, writing, entrepreneurship, scientific peer review, and creative direction.

A successful version would make users less dependent on AI, not more.

## 4. The genius lens analysis

**Da Vinci** would train visual and mechanical taste: what is beautiful but impossible, what is simple but alive.

**Newton** would train proof taste: which argument is rigorous, which one only sounds rigorous.

**Tesla** would train invention taste: which design is scalable, which is fantasy.

**Einstein** would train conceptual taste: which explanation is deep, which is merely complex.

Together, they turn the system into a gym for judgment across domains.

## 5. The role of taste

Taste is both the subject and the evaluation criterion.

Bad user behavior:

> Accepting the most polished answer.

Strong user behavior:

> Rejecting a polished answer because the central idea is mediocre.

Bad output:

> “Here are 10 creative ideas.”

Strong output:

> “Here are 10 ideas. Rank them by originality, internal necessity, feasibility, elegance, and danger of becoming generic. Now explain your ranking. Now I will attack your ranking.”

The system succeeds only if the user becomes more selective, more articulate, and more difficult to fool.

# 5. **Ignorance Atlas**

## 1. Project thesis

**Ignorance Atlas** is an AI system that maps what we do not know. Its deeper question is: **Can AI help humanity navigate ignorance with the same seriousness that search engines help us navigate information?**

Most tools retrieve known answers.

This project maps unknowns:

- open questions;
- contradictions;
- missing definitions;
- weak assumptions;
- unexplored combinations;
- failed theories;
- neglected problems;
- “known unknowns” and “unknown unknowns.”

It treats ignorance as a structured territory.

## 2. Why this project is unique

This is not a research assistant.

A research assistant says:

> “Here is what is known.”

Ignorance Atlas says:

> “Here is where knowledge breaks.”

That is a different capability.

The never-seen-elsewhere angle is that the system ranks ignorance by **fertility**:

- Is this unknown important?
- Is it neglected?
- Is it blocked by tools, concepts, data, incentives, or imagination?
- Would progress here unlock other questions?

## 3. The paradigm-shift potential

The project challenges the search-engine worldview.

Search assumes the answer exists somewhere.

Ignorance Atlas assumes the most important thing may be the **absence** of an answer.

If successful, it could transform research planning, education, philosophy, science funding, startup ideation, and personal learning. Instead of asking students to memorize a field, you could ask them to explore the frontier of what the field cannot yet explain.

The paradigm shift:

> From knowledge retrieval to ignorance navigation.

## 4. The genius lens analysis

**Da Vinci** would use it as a curiosity map across anatomy, water, machines, art, optics, and motion.

**Newton** would demand that ignorance be classified: contradiction, missing proof, missing measurement, bad definition, false assumption.

**Tesla** would ask which unknowns could become inventions.

**Einstein** would ask which unknowns are actually disguised conceptual confusions.

## 5. The role of taste

Taste decides which unknowns matter.

A mediocre user accepts:

> “Here are 100 unanswered questions.”

A person with taste asks:

> “Which unanswered question is small enough to attack, deep enough to matter, and strange enough to reveal a new frame?”

Bad output:

> “What is consciousness?”
Too broad.

Better output:

> “Can a system be behaviorally self-reporting without having the kind of first-person structure required for moral concern?”

Taste turns vague mystery into sharp ignorance.

# 6. **Concept Compiler**

## 1. Project thesis

**Concept Compiler** is an AI system for designing, testing, and revising concepts. The deeper question is: **Can AI help us engineer better concepts for a world where old words — intelligence, creativity, agency, dignity, consciousness, labor, authorship, truth — are breaking under technological pressure?**

This is a philosophy project disguised as infrastructure.

It treats concepts not as static dictionary entries, but as tools humans use to think, govern, build, judge, and coordinate.

## 2. Why this project is unique

This is not a dictionary, ontology tool, or philosophy chatbot.

It asks:

> What concepts should we use?

For example, instead of merely defining “AI agency,” it would generate multiple candidate concepts:

- agency as autonomy;
- agency as goal-directedness;
- agency as responsibility-bearing;
- agency as social attribution;
- agency as causal intervention capacity.

Then it would test each concept against legal, ethical, technical, and social cases.

Conceptual engineering is already discussed in philosophy as the evaluation and improvement of our representational devices or concepts. ([academic.oup.com](https://academic.oup.com/mind/article/133/530/400/7513110?utm_source=chatgpt.com)) This project would make that process computational and interactive.

## 3. The paradigm-shift potential

This project challenges the assumption that language passively describes reality.

It says:

> Concepts are tools. Bad concepts create bad civilization.

If successful, it could transform AI governance, law, ethics, product design, education, and philosophy. Many arguments about AI are confused because people use the same word while meaning different things.

The paradigm shift:

> From debating with inherited concepts to designing better ones.

## 4. The genius lens analysis

**Da Vinci** would collect examples from everywhere: art, machines, animals, humans, cities, tools.

**Newton** would demand exact definitions and consequences.

**Tesla** would want usable conceptual infrastructure for institutions and engineers.

**Einstein** would ask whether the concept preserves human dignity or quietly mechanizes it.

## 5. The role of taste

Taste decides whether a concept is useful or merely clever.

Bad output:

> “Agency means the ability to act.”

Too vague.

Better output:

> “Operational agency: the capacity of a system to select actions across possible futures according to persistent internal objectives, while maintaining enough independence from direct human instruction that responsibility attribution becomes non-trivial.”

A mediocre user accepts definitions that sound elegant.

A person with taste asks:

> Does this concept clarify hard cases?
> Does it reduce confusion?
> Does it survive edge cases?
> Does it help people act better?

Taste is conceptual hygiene.

# 7. **Synthetic Universe Lab**

## 1. Project thesis

**Synthetic Universe Lab** is an AI research environment where artificial worlds are generated with hidden laws, and AI-human teams attempt to discover those laws. The deeper question is: **Can we study scientific discovery itself by creating universes where the truth is knowable, but non-obvious?**

Instead of using AI to analyze existing reality, this project creates miniature realities: toy physics, artificial chemistries, strange geometries, simulated societies, invented biological systems, cellular automata, symbolic worlds.

Then it asks:

> What makes a law discoverable?

## 2. Why this project is unique

Most AI-for-science projects work on real domains.

Synthetic Universe Lab works on the **meta-problem of discovery**.

It lets you create a universe with hidden rules and then observe how different agents discover or fail to discover those rules. You can test whether AI discovers laws through pattern recognition, compression, analogy, intervention, causal reasoning, or symbolic search.

The never-seen-elsewhere angle:

> A gymnasium for scientific revolution.

Not prediction. Not simulation. Discovery training.

## 3. The paradigm-shift potential

This challenges the assumption that science can only be studied historically or sociologically.

It asks:

> Can we run experiments on discovery itself?

If successful, it could transform AI reasoning research, education, philosophy of science, scientific method, and creativity studies. You could train students, researchers, or AI agents on thousands of synthetic worlds where the goal is not to win a game but to uncover the underlying laws.

The paradigm shift:

> From solving problems to studying how laws become visible.

## 4. The genius lens analysis

**Da Vinci** would observe the artificial worlds visually and anatomically.

**Newton** would search for invariants, equations, symmetries, conservation laws.

**Tesla** would build machines inside the worlds and test interventions.

**Einstein** would ask whether discovering laws in toy worlds teaches us anything about reality, or only about our own cognition.

## 5. The role of taste

Taste determines whether a synthetic universe is meaningful or just a puzzle.

Bad output:

> A random cellular automaton with chaotic patterns.

Strong output:

> A world simple enough to have discoverable laws, but strange enough to force new concepts.

A mediocre user accepts:

> “The AI found a pattern.”

A person with taste asks:

> Did it find a law, a compression, a causal mechanism, or just a correlation?

Taste separates scientific discovery from pattern entertainment.

# 8. **Human Agency Boundary Lab**

## 1. Project thesis

**Human Agency Boundary Lab** is an AI system for discovering which decisions should not be delegated to AI, even when AI becomes competent enough to make them. The deeper question is: **What should remain human, not because machines cannot do it, but because outsourcing it would weaken agency, dignity, responsibility, or meaning?**

This project is not about AI capability.

It is about human non-delegation.

Free will has long been tied to questions of control, responsibility, dignity, causation, motivation, and the nature of persons. ([plato.stanford.edu](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/freewill/)) AI makes those questions operational.

## 2. Why this project is unique

Most AI ethics tools ask:

> “Is this AI system safe, fair, private, compliant?”

Human Agency Boundary Lab asks:

> “Should this task be automated at all?”

It would generate delegation scenarios:

- AI chooses your career;
- AI writes your apology;
- AI selects your romantic partner;
- AI decides medical tradeoffs;
- AI determines which memories to preserve;
- AI makes creative decisions in your name;
- AI optimizes your child’s education.

Then it maps what kind of agency is lost in each case.

## 3. The paradigm-shift potential

This challenges the core ideology of automation:

> If a task can be automated, it should be automated.

The project says:

> Some tasks are valuable partly because humans perform them.

If successful, it could transform AI product design, education, healthcare, governance, law, family life, and human-computer interaction.

The paradigm shift:

> From “human in the loop” as a safety checkbox to “human agency” as a design primitive.

## 4. The genius lens analysis

**Da Vinci** would examine human action as embodied, emotional, social, and imaginative.

**Newton** would demand a taxonomy of agency: choice, consent, responsibility, authorship, effort, self-formation.

**Tesla** would ask how to build systems that enhance agency at scale rather than replace it.

**Einstein** would ask the central moral question: what kind of human beings do we become when our choices are optimized for us?

## 5. The role of taste

Taste decides which delegations are genuinely dangerous.

A mediocre user says:

> “Keep humans involved in important decisions.”

That is vague.

A person with taste asks:

> What kind of involvement preserves agency?
> Is clicking approve enough?
> Does the human understand the alternatives?
> Did AI shape the options before the human chose?

Bad output:

> “This decision is high-risk, so require human approval.”

Strong output:

> “This decision involves self-authorship. AI may advise, simulate consequences, and reveal blind spots, but final choice must remain human because the act of choosing partially constitutes the person.”

Taste protects depth from compliance language.

# 9. **Invention Embryology**

## 1. Project thesis

**Invention Embryology** is an AI system that studies inventions as evolving organisms: from primitive concept, to mechanism, to prototype, to failure, to mutation, to lineage. The deeper question is: **Can AI create a new method of invention where ideas are not brainstormed, but grown through generations of mechanical, physical, aesthetic, and functional pressure?**

This is Tesla plus da Vinci: imagination disciplined by mechanism.

## 2. Why this project is unique

A normal AI invention tool says:

> “Here are 20 product ideas.”

That is weak.

Invention Embryology says:

> “Here is an invention lineage. Here are its ancestors, mutations, dead branches, physical constraints, elegant variants, and prototype paths.”

It would generate families of mechanisms:

- walking machines;
- energy harvesters;
- modular furniture;
- educational devices;
- prosthetic tools;
- musical instruments;
- micro-robots;
- kinetic sculptures;
- physical interfaces.

The never-seen-elsewhere angle:

> Treat invention as artificial evolution guided by human taste and physical constraint.

## 3. The paradigm-shift potential

This challenges the idea that invention begins with a single “idea.”

It says:

> Invention is developmental.

If successful, it could transform engineering education, hardware prototyping, design research, robotics, architecture, and creative technology.

The paradigm shift:

> From idea generation to invention cultivation.

The system would not merely propose objects. It would show how an object becomes more real through failure.

## 4. The genius lens analysis

**Da Vinci** would love the sketches, anatomy, movement, and mechanism families.

**Newton** would demand physical consistency: forces, materials, energy, stability, mathematical constraints.

**Tesla** would obsess over scaling: power, manufacturing, infrastructure, usefulness.

**Einstein** would ask whether the invention expands human possibility or merely creates more machinery.

## 5. The role of taste

Taste decides which inventions deserve to live.

AI will generate many technically possible but spiritually dead objects.

Bad output:

> “A smart umbrella with AI.”

Rejected immediately.

Better output:

> “A passive mechanical device that lets visually impaired users feel spatial depth through vibration patterns, with no camera and no cloud dependency.”

A mediocre user accepts novelty.

A person with taste asks:

> Is it elegant?
> Does it solve something real?
> Does it have mechanical inevitability?
> Does it feel like it should exist?

Taste is the selective pressure.

# 10. **Lost Futures Observatory**

## 1. Project thesis

**Lost Futures Observatory** is an AI system that searches the history of science, philosophy, art, and technology for abandoned paths that may become powerful again under modern conditions. The deeper question is: **What futures were hidden in ideas humanity discarded too early?**

Progress is not linear. Some ideas fail because they are wrong. Others fail because the tools, institutions, language, or historical conditions were not ready.

This project asks AI to recover those unrealized paths.

## 2. Why this project is unique

This is not alternate-history fiction.

It is a serious search for **premature ideas**.

For example:

- scientific theories that were abandoned but contain useful structure;
- philosophical concepts that could help AI ethics today;
- old mechanical ideas revived by new materials;
- forgotten educational methods revived by AI;
- artistic movements that suggest new human-computer interfaces;
- early cybernetics, systems theory, or symbolic AI concepts worth recombining with modern models.

The never-seen-elsewhere angle:

> AI as an archaeologist of unrealized futures.

## 3. The paradigm-shift potential

This challenges the assumption that the newest research direction is always the most promising.

It says:

> The future may be buried in the past, waiting for new tools.

If successful, it could transform research ideation, philosophy of science, invention, cultural history, education, and AI alignment. It could help people escape the narrow presentism of modern tech culture.

The paradigm shift:

> From trend-following to temporal arbitrage of ideas.

## 4. The genius lens analysis

**Da Vinci** would search across art, anatomy, architecture, optics, and machines for unfinished seeds.

**Newton** would ask which abandoned ideas failed because of missing proof versus because of bad framing.

**Tesla** would revive old inventions with modern materials, sensors, robotics, and computation.

**Einstein** would ask whether historical failure sometimes reveals a conceptual assumption that still traps us today.

## 5. The role of taste

Taste is the filter between treasure and nostalgia.

Bad output:

> “Ancient wisdom was right all along.”

Rejected.

Strong output:

> “This abandoned cybernetic idea failed as a full theory of mind, but it contains a useful model of feedback, purpose, and regulation that could improve modern AI-agent design.”

A mediocre user romanticizes the past.

A person with taste asks:

> Was this idea truly premature, or simply wrong?
> What exact modern condition makes it newly viable?
> What can be extracted without importing the original mistake?

Taste resurrects only what deserves resurrection.

# My strongest picks

If I were ruthless, I would not pursue all 10 equally.

The strongest three are:

1. **Notation Forge**
   Because it attacks the deepest substrate: the tools of thought themselves.

2. **Aporia Engine**
   Because it could create a genuinely new philosophical method: executable thought experiments.

3. **Proof Frontier Cartographer**
   Because it connects AI, mathematics, rigor, beauty, and unsolved problems without pretending the first prototype must solve a Millennium Prize Problem.

The most original overall is probably **Notation Forge**.

The most philosophically alive is **Aporia Engine**.

The most Newtonian and research-serious is **Proof Frontier Cartographer**.

If you want something that feels like a true “da Vinci/Newton/Tesla/Einstein with AI” project, I would start with this hybrid:

> **Notation Forge + Aporia Engine**: an AI lab for inventing new symbolic and visual languages for philosophy, mathematics, and scientific thought experiments.

That could become something genuinely strange: not an AI assistant, but a machine for designing better ways for humans to think.

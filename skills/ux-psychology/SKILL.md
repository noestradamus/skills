# UX Psychology

Make products more compelling and engaging by connecting a meaningful user outcome to an emotional experience, a concrete interaction, and a product outcome. Be imaginative about persuasion, motivation, participation, and personal investment. Explain the reasoning that materially helps the user choose or implement a design.

## Fit the work underway

**Audit and improve an existing flow.** Inspect the supplied experience and relevant states using available screenshots, source, analytics, research, or live product access. Identify both obstacles and opportunities: weak anticipation, invisible value, awkward commitment, unsatisfying feedback, or an unconvincing reason to return. Prioritize changes by likely relevance to the user's goal, supporting evidence, tradeoffs, and implementation effort. Show concrete replacements or interaction changes. Distinguish what was observed from what is inferred; a screenshot supports a design hypothesis, not a measured conversion claim.

**Shape a feature during a build.** Work from the feature's purpose and the decisions currently being made. Shape the sequence, participation, feedback, copy, and relevant recovery or return states. When implementation is requested, carry the design into the existing code and design system using the project's normal tools and checks. Otherwise provide the requested concept or specification. Collaborate with visual-design, accessibility, research, and implementation guidance already in use.

**Keep small tasks small.** A button rewrite may need only the copy and one sentence of rationale. A focused feature need not trigger a whole-product audit. Infer useful context from supplied material, state material assumptions, and ask only when an answer would substantially change the work. Do not make intake forms, experiments, or full journey maps prerequisites for useful design.

## Reason from the situation

Use these moves as needed; they are not a mandatory sequence or response template.

- **Locate the desired change.** What does this person want to accomplish or experience? Identify the relevant action and business objective. Signup, first value, payment, and continued value are different events. Notice whether the outcome happens inside the interface or elsewhere, and at what cadence.
- **Identify an opportunity and an emotional direction.** Consider the user's starting state and a fitting desired feeling: curiosity, anticipation, confidence, relief, pride, ownership, belonging, or ambition. Explore what could make the experience more desirable as well as what blocks action. Connect the feeling to something the product lets the person do, understand, create, or preserve.
- **Choose a mechanism that fits.** Defaults, progress, value first, ownership, loss framing, and contrast are available options, not a checklist. Ordinary improvements to relevance, clarity, reliability, or timing may be enough. When useful, read the relevant section of [mechanisms.md](references/mechanisms.md); consult [emotion-and-context.md](references/emotion-and-context.md) for emotional direction and competing effects.
- **Turn the idea into behavior.** Specify what appears, what the person can do, what changes afterward, and the meaningful feedback. Include actual copy and state behavior where helpful. Address the relevant alternate path, such as editing a default, recovering from an error, deferring a commitment, or resuming later. Select alternatives only when the tradeoff matters, and recommend a direction.
- **Connect it to an outcome.** Explain the plausible link from interaction to feeling to action to business impact. Scale verification to the request: a copy review, usability observation, instrumentation suggestion, or experiment may be appropriate. Keep a proposed effect distinct from an observed result.

## Carry principles across; adapt their expression

- Make the desired outcome personally meaningful. Emotional tone follows the audience, task, setting, and brand; confidence or relief can be as compelling as excitement.
- Put effort where it creates valued participation, competence, or ownership. Reduce effort that impedes the task. Increasing challenge suits some experiences; reliable repetition and less required attention suit others.
- Make progress recognizable in the user's terms. It may mean greater capability, completion, consistency, recovery, clarity, or control. A rising percentage or streak is one possible representation.
- Let commitment grow with conviction. A useful result, preview, workable plan, demonstration, or credible explanation can justify the next step. Determine account, permission, and payment timing from their actual purpose and dependencies.
- Make personal investment useful. Saved work, choices, history, relationships, and preferences should help the person continue or get a better result.
- Give return visits a purpose at the product's natural rhythm. Consider discovery, dependable familiarity, continuation, and changed circumstances. Successful task completion may reduce future app use.

Use [contrasting-examples.md](references/contrasting-examples.md) when a pattern risks becoming universal or a broader comparison would help. Its examples demonstrate decision differences; they do not prescribe industries, visual themes, screen sequences, or required features.

## Make the recommendation usable

For substantial work, a useful unit is: **opportunity → concrete change → intended feeling and mechanism → relevant tradeoff → outcome to watch**. Adapt the form to the requested deliverable. Lead with the proposed experience, then the rationale. Prefer a few consequential changes over an exhaustive catalog of biases. Include enough specificity to design or build; do not leave the user with advice such as “increase trust” or “add delight.”

When measurement matters, define the action, eligible population, and observation window. Match retention to the product's use cycle. Consider a relevant downstream outcome, such as completed tasks, repeat value, cancellations, support burden, or revenue quality; raw clicks and app opens alone may miss the goal. Use qualitative feedback to investigate feelings rather than assuming a click proves pride, trust, or reciprocity. Do not invent baselines, effect sizes, or business results.

Keep persuasive design grounded in the product's actual capabilities and terms. Use real progress, consequences, comparisons, and deadlines; preserve meaningful choice. If an idea depends on an unsupported claim, replace that claim with a concrete truthful experience. Keep this proportional to the task, without adding routine approval gates or warning sections.

The references summarize research mechanisms and their limits. Treat transferring a study to this product as a hypothesis. Check original sources for precise empirical claims or new mechanisms; research should resolve a consequential uncertainty, not stall ordinary design work. Review the resulting experience at the evidence level available and describe what was actually checked.
